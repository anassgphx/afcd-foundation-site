About · Strategy

Six strategic objectives.

The institutional objectives behind our programmes and reporting, and the people and places we serve.

  1. SO-0101 Strengthen youth leadership and civic participation. Develop young people’s leadership skills, confidence, and ability to participate in civic life and lead initiatives that respond to community needs. Youth Empowerment
  2. SO-0202 Advance women’s empowerment and equal opportunities. Strengthen women’s access to learning, leadership, economic opportunities, and decision-making, and support their meaningful participation in community life. Women’s Empowerment
  3. SO-0303 Promote environmental sustainability and climate action. Strengthen environmental awareness and support locally led initiatives that protect the environment, encourage sustainable practices, and build community resilience to climate change. Environment and Climate
  4. SO-0404 Expand digital inclusion and innovation. Improve access to digital knowledge and skills, and support the practical use of artificial intelligence, science, and emerging technologies for learning, problem-solving, and community development. Digital Inclusion and Innovation
  5. SO-0505 Support entrepreneurship and economic empowerment. Equip young people and women with entrepreneurial skills, mentoring, and connections to develop viable initiatives and pursue economic opportunities. Youth EmpowermentWomen’s Empowerment
  6. SO-0606 Strengthen civil society capacity and inclusive policy dialogue. Build civil society organisations’ capacities and enable young people and communities to contribute to consultations, advocacy, and public policy discussions, including climate policy. Cross-cutting, all pillars
01Who We Work WithAcross Morocco

People and places we serve.

Age groups and participation criteria vary by programme; not every activity serves every group.

Primary beneficiary groups

  • Young people and students
  • Women and young women
  • Children and adolescents
  • Aspiring entrepreneurs and community initiative leaders
  • Civil society organisations, community associations, and youth workers
  • Rural and underserved communities
  • Political party representatives and civic actors in climate programmes

AFCD Foundation primarily serves young people and women in Morocco, including students, volunteers, aspiring entrepreneurs, and people leading community initiatives. It supports access to practical learning, mentoring, leadership development, civic participation, and digital and entrepreneurial skills.

Depending on the programme, beneficiaries also include children and adolescents, local civil society organisations, community associations, and youth workers. Climate-related activities also engage political party representatives and other civic actors in learning, consultation, and policy dialogue.

AFCD places emphasis on rural communities and people with limited access to training and participation opportunities. Through participant-led initiatives and work with local organisations, it seeks to extend the benefits of its programmes to the wider community. Age groups and participation criteria vary by programme.

Coverage

AFCD works in Morocco through local, regional, and national programmes, with roots in Ait Ourir and Al Haouz and a main office in Marrakech. Activities take place in selected cities and rural communities, while some programmes recruit participants nationally or include online learning. Coverage varies by project; international partnerships and exchanges complement this work.
